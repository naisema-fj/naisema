The idea

Whose body part? Which group?

In Fijian, a body-part word can take a possessive ending. We will look at the relationship first, then choose a form. These examples follow Vosa Vakaviti Raraba and still need qualified speaker review before public teaching.

01 / FORM

Attach to a body word

bati+-qu→batiqu

Taukeni kuri: a checked example for “my teeth.” Compare na noqu vale (“my house”): noqu is before vale. It is not the default before every body word.

02 / PEOPLE

Count the people

●●●●

One, two, a few, or many changes the ending. “A few” does not have to mean exactly three.

03 / VIEWPOINT

Is the listener in the group?

speakerlistenerothers

Inclusive means the speaker and listener are together. Exclusive means the listener is outside the speaker’s group.

How this preview treats the language

The original video supplies the starting word list. Some labels can name more than one nearby part, and not every body word takes the same possession pattern. This preview keeps those differences visible while a qualified Fijian speaker reviews the final words and examples.
