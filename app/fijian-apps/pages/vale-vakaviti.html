    01 · FOLLOW THE FORCE

    Where might the wind find a loose link?

    At a roof edge, can you trace a possible path from through and toward ?

    1. If wind pulls upward, which joint would you question first? What in this frame supports your guess?
    2. If you strengthen that joint with a new connector, where might the force travel next? Could the older joining idea teach you how to keep the whole path connected?
    3. How would you compare two small models under repeated pulling and pushing before asking a qualified builder to judge the full design?
    02 · REWORK THE ROOF

    What can the triangle teach the next roof?

    What changes when you compare the front sloping timbers with the and the interior candidate?

    1. Where could you add bracing without closing the stair-facing entrance? Can you sketch two routes for the force and compare them?
    2. If you shortened an eave to reduce wind exposure, what might happen to shade and rain protection? What else could solve both needs?
    3. Which part of your proposal could a model show, and which claim would need local wind-load calculations and an engineer's review?
    03 · KEEP THE RELATIONSHIPS

    What should a stronger house still mean?

    What might you learn by comparing the interior candidate, the front opening, and the side of the frame?

    1. Whose local account would help you understand how the front and side entrances are used here? What might differ in another community?
    2. If a modern connector changes what people see or how they enter, which cultural meaning could change with it? Who should help decide?
    3. Could local builders repair your idea after a storm with available skills and materials? What would you revise after their answer?
