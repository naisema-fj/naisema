## Phase 1a product scope

- **Content types in 1a:** Page, Article (E-zine), Episode (Voices), Resource, Provider and Offering (plain listings), Creator Profile with one free sample, Topic, and video Content Items with Learning Layers.
- **Deferred to 1b:** Radio Station and Radio Programme with timetables, Marketplace listing (MKT-01), E-zine Issue, featured slots, richer Offering filters.
- **Language Variety for the Seed Collection:** Standard Fijian only. Variety is still recorded on every Learning Layer and language Review Approval.
- **Consultation (GOV-01, GOV-02):** an approved external survey tool and an access-controlled Na iSema-owned spreadsheet register in 1a; storage location and deletion documented under DATA-04.
- **Reports and data requests (SAFE-03, DATA-03):** one in-app Case queue covering reports, rights concerns and data requests.
- **Search (PUB-03):** D1 full-text search (FTS5) built from eligible Revisions.
- **Rich text:** article and page bodies are stored as Tiptap/ProseMirror JSON with a small fixed block set (headings, lists, links, quotes, images with required alt text, embedded Content Items, callouts); exported as JSON and HTML.
- **Analytics:** Cloudflare Web Analytics (cookieless) for traffic; Workers Analytics Engine for server-side product events carrying item/area IDs only, never learner IDs. Privacy adviser to confirm whether a consent banner is needed.

## Data and age policy (PRD §34 decision 4)

- **Accounts:** 18+ self-declared Learner Accounts only; no under-18 accounts.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare with Oceania location hints; overseas processing disclosed (ADR-0004).
- **Learner data:** history clearing is immediate; account deletion removes live data immediately and leaves backups within 35 days; accounts inactive for 24 months are deleted after a 30-day warning email; self-service JSON export from the private learning page.
- **Retention defaults:** PRD §11 defaults adopted (unsuccessful educator evidence 90 days; routine enquiries 12 months; identifiable consultation data 12 months after analysis). Closed Cases kept 2 years after closure, then reviewed for deletion or de-identification. Audit events kept for the life of their object plus 2 years. All subject to privacy adviser confirmation.
- **Backups:** D1 Time Travel (30 days) plus nightly D1 export to a private R2 bucket kept 35 days; deletion ledger replayed after any restore (ADR-0009); restore drill on staging before launch.

## Video implementation decisions (PRD §34 "Video implementation decisions")

- **Seed Collection:** three clips of 30–120 seconds, at least one vertical and one landscape, across at least two everyday or cultural contexts; Standard Fijian.
- **Upload limits:** 2 GB and 15 minutes per source master; MP4 and MOV only, validated at upload start. Longer sources are used through Excerpts.
- **Caption authoring:** Segments are the source of truth; WebVTT import/export; machine transcripts only as unreviewed drafts (ADR-0008).
- **Default Completion Rule:** every required activity attempted with feedback viewed, or its accessible equivalent; viewing alone never completes; real-world use is optional.
- **Cost envelope:** about AUD 100/month ceiling for 1a, with Cloudflare billing alerts at 50% and 80% and media usage visible in the VAC-10 cost report.
- **Test profile:** PRD profile (1.5 Mbps down, 150 ms latency, five cold runs, first frame within 5 s in at least 4) on one mid-range Android (Moto G / Samsung A class) and one older iPhone (SE class), plus Playwright with the same throttling in CI on player changes. Exact device models to be recorded here.

## Interim decisions during the 1a build

- **Rights evidence before the scan pipeline (1a-06, 30 September 2026):** until the quarantine and ClamAV pipeline exists (issue #14, ADR-0010), rights evidence is stored straight in the private `EVIDENCE` bucket. To limit the risk it must be a PDF, JPEG, PNG or WebP of at most 10 MB whose first bytes, declared type and file extension agree; it is served only to editors, only as a sandboxed download that is never cached; and every download is audited. #14 must route rights evidence through quarantine like every other upload. **Done in 1a-09 (1 October 2026):** evidence is now quarantined and scanned like every upload, and can't be downloaded until it passes. Evidence recorded before then stays where it is, unscanned.
- **What a Rights Record covers before the media library (1a-06):** a Content Item's own Rights Record covers everything in it, including images in an Article body. Media assets get their own Rights Records, checked by `isEligible`, when the media library arrives (#14). **Done (1 October 2026, completing #17):** every media library file an item uses needs a current Rights Record of its own granting Publish; the item's record covers its words and images from other sites.
- **Public site design (1a-07, 1 October 2026):** the "Letters home" direction (`DESIGN.md`) follows PRD §28. It adds two self-hosted typefaces, Jost and Literata (SIL Open Font License 1.1). **Founder approval of these components under MOU §5 is still to be recorded here.** The footer's masi-style strip is a labelled placeholder until commissioned, culturally reviewed artwork replaces it.
- **Production origin (1a-07):** cache purges for production assume the public site is served at `https://naisema.com` (`PUBLIC_ORIGINS` in `wrangler.jsonc`). Change it if production uses another hostname, such as `www.naisema.com`.
- **Edge cache freshness (1a-07):** until a zone purge token is set (runbook, "Public site"), a withdrawn page can still be served from another Cloudflare data centre for up to 5 minutes. Eligibility is still checked on every uncached request.
- **Search, listings and the index (1a-08, 1 October 2026):** search, area pages, the homepage and the sitemap all read one FTS5 index. The index is rebuilt for an item on every public change, and daily for expired rights. A SQL pre-filter on current Publish rights keeps counts, Topic options and the sitemap up to date. Every item shown on a page is re-checked by the eligibility decision (ADR-0007). The sitemap is not re-checked, because it only suggests addresses and each page enforces eligibility itself. Area pages list the newest 20 and link to search for the rest, so cached pages never depend on a query string. The `search_performed` event records IDs, filters and counts only. A search page runs the eligibility decision for up to 20 items, about 165 D1 queries, which is within the Workers Paid limit of 1,000 per request but not the Free plan's 50.
- **Search words in request logs (1a-08):** search words are in the URL, so they don't reach analytics, but they do appear in Workers Logs request URLs (`observability` in `wrangler.jsonc`) for Cloudflare's log retention period. The privacy adviser should confirm this is acceptable, or the logs should drop query strings, as part of #10.
- **D1 export and FTS5 (1a-08):** D1 export does not support virtual tables. Backups (#34) must use Time Travel, or drop and rebuild `search_fts` around an export.
- **Media delivery before media Rights Records (1a-09):** a media library file is served to anyone with its unguessable address as soon as it passes its scan. Its own Rights Records come later; the library shows a placeholder until then. Images and PDFs are only reachable through content that links them. Delivered files are edge-cached for up to 5 minutes and nothing purges them yet, because a ready file can't be withdrawn yet; withdrawing media must purge `/media/...` when it arrives. **Done (completing #17):** a file is delivered only while a Rights Record of its own grants Publish, and a change to its records purges its addresses and the items using it. A file shows publicly only once its rights are recorded, so editors record them before using the file. Delivery asks the same rule as `isEligible` (`isPublishable` in `rights-rules.ts`), one level down: a file has no review or publication state of its own, only rights. Each item shown that uses media library files costs two more D1 queries, so a search page can now exceed the "about 165" counted for 1a-08; still well within Workers Paid. The daily job reindexes every item using a file whose record expired.
- **Scanner signatures and internet access (1a-09):** the scanner container keeps internet access so freshclam can update ClamAV's signatures; the image is rebuilt with fresh signatures on every deploy. ClamAV reports files it can't fully scan (over its size limits, encrypted, broken executables), and those are refused rather than passed. That includes password-protected PDFs.
- **Resources, Pages and Topic pages (1a-18, 1 October 2026):** Resources and Pages share the Article's Revision model and editor rather than having their own, and the admin section is now "Content". Resource files are limited to PDF and audio (MP3, M4A) from the media library, scanned clean, and are downloaded only through their Resource, which decides eligibility on every download (ADR-0007); audio has no media address of its own. Video Resources wait for the video pipeline (#16). Pages are one per footer page and sit outside the six areas. Topics allow one level of subtopics. `content_opened` is counted with a beacon image, because public pages run no JavaScript and are cached at the edge; it records the item ID only, and only while the item is eligible. Anyone can request the beacon, so view counts are indicative, not audited. A Resource's "usage terms" are plain words for visitors, distinct from the Permitted Uses a Rights Record grants (CONTEXT.md). Broken-link reports keep no addresses, only an HMAC of the IP address and the day (keyed with `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`, so rotating it resets that day's de-duplication).
- **Voices Episodes (1a-19, 1 October 2026):** Episodes are a content type on the shared Revision model and always sit in the Voices area, whose page is the series landing page. 1a ships audio Episodes only; video Episodes come with the video pipeline (#16, blocked by the #4 Stream spike). The "reviewed transcript" of A11Y-03 is an accessibility review that every Episode needs. It covers the audio and the transcript, and the public Review Label reads "Transcript reviewed for accessibility". Audio is streamed through the Episode with byte ranges and is never cached, so a withdrawal or lapsed right stops it at once, at the cost of repeat downloads. An Episode's Revision lists its speakers, music and archive clips, and a Rights Record can cover one of those parts. The Episode still needs its own record, and each part the published Revision lists that has records needs a current one granting Publish; cutting a part in a new Revision lifts its rights rather than leaving the Episode blocked by a record that can't be deleted. Distribution links are part of the Revision, so the editor who publishes it approves them, and an editorial review covers them whenever one is required. There is no RSS feed (§9).
- **Providers, Offerings and Creator Profiles (1a-20, 1 October 2026):** the listings live under **Connect**, not Discover as #26 first said. The founder chose this on 1 October 2026, because Connect is "Classes, courses and people who teach". Providers and Offerings are plain audited listings (decision: content types in 1a), with no Revisions or reviews. Creator Profiles are Content Items, so consent, the portrait's rights and review go through the usual gates. "Partner" depends only on a recorded Partnership Agreement in force; the agreement records where the signed document is kept, not the document itself. Authorised embeds are shown as labelled links in 1a: embedding another site's page would need a per-site `frame-src` in the content security policy, and is left until a Partner asks for it. A Creator's free sample must be a public Na iSema item; every Na iSema item is free in 1a. When an item changes, the items showing it (related, embedded, or as a sample) are refreshed one level deep, which replaces the 5-minute wait accepted for related items in 1a-18. Partnership Agreement days are Fiji's (UTC+12), the calendar Na iSema's agreements are made in. Creator Profiles carry no sponsorship or feature fields: they are Content Items, featured the way other content is (a Topic's lead feature), and sponsored creators aren't part of 1a; a Provider's or Offering's sponsor is always disclosed.
- **Public forms, Submissions and Consent Records (1a-21, 2 October 2026):** the EDU-01 educator categories aren't written down in this repository, so 1a uses "First-language or fluent speaker", "Qualified language teacher", "Community, church or cultural educator", "Linguist or researcher" and "Something else" (`EDUCATOR_CATEGORIES` in `app/lib/submission-fields.ts`). Replace them if the PRD's list differs. Three consent purposes each have their own notice and Consent Record: replying to what was sent (needed on every form), being contacted about consultations (needed on the consultation form), and the newsletter (optional). Their first wordings, in migration 0011, follow the retention defaults above; **the privacy adviser should confirm them (#10)**, and the privacy contact publishes changes at `/admin/notices`. A Submission is due 7 days after it arrives, in Fiji's days, which editors can change; an upload link works for 7 days and takes at most 20 files. Forms allow 5 sends a minute per address and form. Pages reached only through an emailed token (withdrawing a consent, an upload link) aren't public forms and have no Turnstile: the token is their protection. Anyone can unsubscribe an address, as with any newsletter, which also withdraws its newsletter Consent Records; the harm is a missed newsletter, and signing up again restores it. If the newsletter tool can't be reached, the newsletter consent just given is removed and the person is told they aren't signed up. Turnstile's widget is the only script a form page runs. The newsletter tool is Buttondown, to be documented under DATA-04. Staging writes newsletter sign-ups to a table rather than sending them. Someone who unsubscribes through Buttondown's own link is not yet marked withdrawn in their Consent Record, because that needs Buttondown's webhook; the privacy contact reconciles the two on request. Consultation sign-ups feed the external survey tool and spreadsheet register decided above, which are not replaced.
- **Cases (1a-22, 2 October 2026):** SAFE-03's "reviewed/closed" is an appeal decided by someone who didn't make the decision, which closes the Case. Appeals are open for 30 days after a decision and taken once; with only one safeguarding lead, an appeal waits for the backup (#5). The outcome a person is told is chosen from fixed wordings, and the case team's notes on what was done and why are never sent. Reports can be anonymous, and anonymous reporters can't be told the outcome. Hiding pending review is a hold that makes the item ineligible (ADR-0007), so a hidden item can't be republished until the safeguarding lead lifts the hold. In 1a a data request is handled by hand: the Case shows the privacy contact what is held for the address, and exports and deletions follow #32 and #34. The community standards' own words are a Page for the founder to write, as for the other footer pages. The reporting route is always shown beneath it.
- **Video asset pipeline (1a-10, 4 October 2026):** a Video Asset is a scanned master in a private `VIDEO_MASTERS` bucket, plus the copy Stream made of it. It shares its ID with the media library upload it came from (CONTEXT.md). The 15-minute limit is enforced on the file itself. The scan step reads the movie header only after ClamAV passes the file, so no unscanned bytes are parsed (ADR-0010). Stream's own measurement is checked again when it reports the video ready. The browser's check only saves an upload that would be refused. The limit is judged to the second, so a video that reads 900.02 seconds passes. Stream is reached through its REST API behind a small provider interface (`video-provider.server.ts`), not the newer Workers Stream binding, so tests can stand in for it and another provider can be pointed at the same masters. Playback tokens are signed in the Worker with a Stream signing key, as Stream recommends above 1,000 tokens a day, and last ten minutes. Stream fetches masters from pre-signed R2 addresses that last one hour. The R2 key behind them is read-only and limited to the masters bucket. If Stream accepts a copy but the answer is lost, a retried scan can ask for a second copy. The first then sits in Stream unrecorded: it can be found by its `meta.naisemaAssetId` and deleted in the dashboard. This is rare enough not to need an idempotency scheme. Webhook signatures are checked, but old timestamps aren't refused: reports only move a video forwards, so a replay can't change anything, and refusing one could lose a report Stream retried late. One Stream account has one webhook address, so it points at production, and staging catches up through the daily check (runbook, Video). Stream's secrets aren't required at deploy yet, because there is no Stream account; until they are set, videos fail with a plain reason. **Video Episodes**, carried over from #25, need a public player. That player must refresh short-lived tokens with JavaScript on otherwise script-free public pages. They therefore move to the learner player (#29), which builds that; the pipeline, Video Assets and staff preview they need are here. #4's Stream-specific checks (Stream's own encodes, H.264, Safari, real phones) are still open and need a Stream account.
- **Learning Layer authoring: Segments and the timeline editor (1a-11, 4 October 2026):**
  - **Videos:** a Video is a new Content Item type showing one Video Asset that has finished processing. Its footage needs a Rights Record of its own, like any media library file. A Video can't be published yet, because its public page plays it with the learner player (#29).
  - **Revisions:** Learning Layers keep their own parent rows and write-once Revisions (`learning_layer`, `learning_layer_revision`), separate from Content Item Revisions, so their numbering and review stay their own (ADR-0006).
  - **Assignment:** editors assign Educators to a Video. An assigned Educator adds Learning Layers to it and is assigned to the ones they add. Editors can add or remove Educators on any Learning Layer, and can open and edit every one. Only a layer's assigned Educators can open it, and a refused attempt is audited (VAC-05).
  - **Language Variety:** every Learning Layer teaches Standard Fijian in 1a, recorded on the layer.
  - **Segments:** times are relative to the clip; an Excerpt's in time is the offset back to the video. Changing the Excerpt keeps each Segment on the same moment of the video and flags those that no longer fit as "Retimed" until their times are edited. Validation runs in the editor as people type and again on save. A Segment needs its Fijian text; its English translation can follow.
  - **WebVTT:** import happens in the browser, so nothing is saved until the Educator saves. A Fijian file replaces the Segments, and an English file fills the translations of the Segments with the same ID or times (within 250 ms). Imported text is an unreviewed draft until someone marks it checked. Export uses the saved draft, with the Segment IDs as cue IDs.
  - **Fingerprints:** language review covers the Fijian, English and Variety; accessibility covers them with their times. Retiming alone keeps a language approval, and marking a draft as checked changes no fingerprint.
  - **Editor:** it needs JavaScript, as the Article editor does. A waveform view is left for later.
- **Tokens, Expressions and Annotations (1a-12, 4 October 2026):**
  - **Tokens:** a Segment's Fijian is split into words. Letters with their accents and macrons, and digits, count as word characters; apostrophes and hyphens join a word; spaces and punctuation aren't tokens. Each token has an ID. A hyphenated compound such as "vale-ni-vuli" is split into its parts, one token each, so a part can be annotated on its own and the whole compound as a run of tokens; it is still shown as written, hyphens and all. Hyphen use in Standard Fijian writing varies, so the Language Reviewer is to confirm this rule (#7); it was settled before any Annotations existed, because changing it later changes token IDs. After an edit the words are matched against the old ones with a longest-common-subsequence diff, ignoring capitals, so words still there keep their IDs; a respelt word is a new word. The editor re-tokenises once an edit to the Fijian is finished, against the tokens from before it, never after each keystroke, because a half-typed word in between could match the wrong word. The server checks the tokens and re-tokenises against the Revision before when they don't match the text. When a word appears twice in a row, the words alone can't show which copy an edit meant. The later copy keeps the ID: a word typed in front of the same word is the new one. Rather than trust that guess, an edit that changes how often an annotated word appears in a Segment flags the Annotation, listed under "Annotations and notes to check" until the Educator confirms it ("It's on the right word") or moves it. The flag, like the vocabulary choice, is authoring state and isn't covered by any fingerprint.
  - **Revalidation:** an Annotation points at a first and last token in one Segment. When either token, or the Segment, is gone, the Annotation is kept exactly as it was and listed for the Educator to select the words again or remove it (ADR-0011). Saving isn't blocked, so no work is lost; the review gates (#28) decide whether flagged Annotations can be approved.
  - **Expressions:** they live in a library per Language Variety. An Expression defined while annotating joins the library when the Learning Layer is saved, if an Annotation uses it, unless one saying exactly the same (word or phrase, meanings, grammar note and pronunciation, case aside) is already there, which is reused instead. A new Expression's ID that is already another's is refused, never swapped, so no Annotation is moved to a different Expression. Each Revision keeps a copy of the Expressions its Annotations use, so it records exactly what was reviewed and exports can be rebuilt from it (VAC-09). A later change in the library reaches a Learning Layer when it is next saved, and changes its fingerprints then. Editors can change any Expression (`expression.edit`). The Educator who added one can change it only while no Learning Layer they aren't assigned to uses it, so one Educator's change never reaches another's work unseen. A refused attempt is audited. A change has no review of its own: it reaches a Learning Layer only when that layer is next saved, where it changes the fingerprints and so needs that layer's review again. Until then, that layer's editor lists the change ("Updated in the library since this Learning Layer was saved"), old wording against new. Language Reviewers approving Expressions directly can come with the review gates (#28).
  - **Idioms:** an idiom needs its literal meaning as well, and it must differ from what the idiom means (VID-07).
  - **Context Notes:** a note sits on the whole Learning Layer or one Segment and needs who the knowledge comes from (VID-12). A note whose Segment is removed is kept and shown with the whole-layer notes until it is moved or removed.
  - **Vocabulary List:** built from the Annotations the Educator marks for it, each Expression once, with every moment it occurs.
  - **Fingerprints:** language review covers Annotations and the Expression copies; cultural review also covers the notes. Token IDs and the vocabulary choice aren't covered.
- **Activities and the Completion Rule (1a-13, 4 October 2026):**
  - **Kinds:** listen and repeat, comprehension (multiple choice), listening discrimination, "what would you say next?" and the optional real-world prompt. Each is on one Segment or the whole clip, except listen and repeat, which always needs the Segment to repeat. Each stores a prompt, its reviewed answers (choices marked correct) or a model response, feedback, whether it is required and a text alternative. Multiple choice and listening discrimination need two to six choices with at least one correct. "What would you say next?" needs choices, a model response or both. A real-world prompt's feedback and example are optional. What each kind needs is one table (`KIND_RULES` in `app/lib/activities.ts`). Changing an Activity's kind drops what the new kind doesn't use (choices, model response, pronunciation), on the server too, so nothing hidden is saved or reviewed. A Learning Layer holds up to 50 Activities, and a link that can't be a Segment is refused. Activities and their choices keep their IDs across Revisions, as Segments do.
  - **Pronunciation and speaking:** listen and repeat carries pronunciation guidance the Educator writes or starts from the pronunciation of the Expressions annotated in that Segment. Saving the Activity is the Educator's approval of it, and language review then covers it. Learners say it aloud and compare with the model; nothing records them. Every page now sends `Permissions-Policy: camera=(), microphone=(), geolocation=()`, so no page can ask for the microphone (VID-08).
  - **Completion Rule:** the default and only rule in 1a is the one the Educator sets by marking Activities required: every required Activity attempted with its feedback viewed, by the standard route or its text alternative, which count the same (`completionProgress` in `app/lib/activities.ts`, for the learner flow in #31). Watching isn't an input, so it never completes a Learning Layer. A Learning Layer with nothing required can't be completed, and the editor says so; saving isn't blocked, and #28 can make it a publishing condition. A real-world prompt can never be required: the editor offers no checkbox for it and a save that marks one is refused. Whether answers were right doesn't count, so correctness stays a separate state.
  - **Revalidation:** an Activity whose Segment is removed is kept and listed until it is linked to another Segment or the whole clip, as notes are.
  - **Preview:** each Activity has a learner preview in the editor, with its text version, which shows the Completion Rule being met. By the text route, writing the answer is the attempt; aloud, the learner says when they have said it, since nothing listens. Changing an Activity, or switching route, starts its preview again. Nothing done in it is saved. Moving Activities up and down, and trying one again in the preview, come with it; retries for learners are #31's.
  - **Fingerprints:** language review covers every Activity's words and answers, including which Segment it is on, since the answers are checked against that line. Cultural review covers them too, because feedback and model responses can carry context. Accessibility covers them, because the text route shows them all. Safeguarding covers the real-world prompts, which ask learners to do something in the world. Which Activities are required, and their order, are editorial only.

- **Learning Layer review, Review Links and publishing (1a-14, 4 October 2026):**
  - **Rows of their own:** Learning Layer Revisions are submitted, assigned, approved and carried forward in their own tables (`learning_layer_submission`, `learning_layer_review_assignment`, `learning_layer_approval`, write-once), by the same pure rules as Content Items (`review-rules.ts`), so their review stays separate from their Video's (ADR-0001, ADR-0006). Who may decide is shared code too; the queries and inserts are written for each set of tables, since the tables differ. Approvals carry forward when a save leaves a Review Type's fingerprint unchanged; editing a reviewed translation needs language review again.
  - **What is required:** a Learning Layer always needs language review in its Variety. It needs a Knowledge Holder Approval when flagged culturally sensitive, its only Content Flag. One on a Video flagged culturally sensitive (in its current draft or its published Revision) starts flagged, and can't be published unflagged, so the flag can't be dropped to skip the Knowledge Holder. Removing the flag needs the review it guarded, as for Content Items. Editors and the assigned Educators can submit (§11).
  - **Readiness:** besides its reviews, a Learning Layer publishes only with Segments, no unreviewed drafts or retimed Segments, no Annotations, notes or Activities that lost their place, and at least one required Activity (decision log 1a-13). These are eligibility reasons, not save errors, so drafts stay saveable.
  - **Rights:** each of Publish, Translate, Transcribe and Educational adaptation, plus Excerpt for one on an Excerpt, must come from a current Rights Record on the Video as a whole. Records for parts don't count. Each use may come from a different record, so withdrawing a record granting only teaching uses removes the Learning Layer but not the Video, and withdrawing the Publish grant removes both (VID-01). The footage file needs its own Publish grant, and footage with identifiable children needs guardian permission, as for the Video. A Case hold on the Video holds its Learning Layers too. Eligibility is evaluated on every request, so a withdrawal takes effect at once (ADR-0007).
  - **Its Video's publication:** a Learning Layer is published and withdrawn on its own, and its eligibility doesn't require its Video to be published, since Videos can't be published until the learner player (#29). The learner player serves a Learning Layer only on an eligible Video's page.
  - **Review Links:** a Review Link points at one exact Learning Layer Revision: the latest, once submitted. Its token is random rather than signed, so it needs no signing key and is revoked by its row; only a hash of it is stored, so the address is shown once. It lasts 14 days and can be revoked, and a database trigger allows nothing else to change. Every opening is logged, as is the start of each play of its video and any attempt on an expired or revoked link (time and outcome only, nothing about the person). The page is served on the public site with `Cache-Control: private, no-store` and `noindex`, watermarked "Draft for review" as the ticket words it (a Review Link shows an unpublished Revision; CONTEXT.md otherwise avoids "draft" for a Revision). It plays the video through the provider's signed address, or in development through a link-checked route serving the master; an Excerpt plays from its in time where the browser plays the file itself, and the page says which part of the video the Learning Layer uses. Rights often come after review, so missing rights don't stop the video (the editor is warned before sharing footage with no Rights Record granting Publish), but footage whose Publish grant was withdrawn, or a Video hidden pending a Case, never plays through a Review Link. Review Links for Content Items aren't built: Knowledge Holder Approvals on them stay as they were in 1a-05.
  - **Knowledge Holder Approvals:** an editor who didn't write or edit the revision records who approved, how, the Review Link they saw it through (which must be for that revision and have been opened), conditions, and optional evidence. The evidence is quarantined, scanned and kept in the EVIDENCE bucket, readable by editors only (`approvalEvidence.read`), and each read is audited; if the approval can't be written, the evidence is refused, as for Rights Records.
  - **Queues (VCMS-06):** editors see Learning Layers waiting for review (with anything else they still need), approved but held up (with why), ready to publish (a withdrawn one again too), and held up by a video that failed processing. They are worked out live on each request, which 1a's few Learning Layers allow; a larger catalogue would want them stored. A video's page lists the Learning Layers on it, and a Video's rights page lists those that rely on its rights. Reviewers' queues include Learning Layers.
